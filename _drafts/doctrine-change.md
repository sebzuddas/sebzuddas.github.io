The math of modern air defense is broken. We are currently witnessing a massive "defense economics" imbalance that should be a wake-up call for the West. Specially for the smaller democracies like Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania with less money to spend.

In the first 96 hours of the recent conflict, the numbers tell a stark story:

943 Patriot interceptors (PAC-3 MSE) were fired in just 4 days.
Production Gap: That represents 18 months of total production (at 50 units/month) vanished in 96 hours.

We are spending ~$5 billion in interceptors to neutralize ~$9 million worth of Iranian-made Shahed-136 drones.

That is a 500:1 cost ratio in favor of the attacker.

The real danger isn't the individual drone; it's the swarm. By forcing a Patriot battery to empty its magazine on $20k drones, the airspace is left wide open for high-speed ballistic missiles. The Shahed isn't just a weapon; it’s a "magazine-clearing" tool.

We cannot win a war of attrition with 20th-century cost structures. The future of defense must be autonomous, scalable, and—above all—economically sustainable.

While many might assume the solution to be a change in miltary doctrine, one that emphasises less capable weapons that can be mass produced, this too carries too much risk. Risk that, if a military relies too much on mass produced weapons, it becomes vulnerable to the high-quality precision weapons of today's era. 

The solution lies not in committing to any one, but rather having a flexible and adaptible enough defence manufacturing system that can switch between high quality and mass produced weapons at will. This is why we are building resilinet, because we see that the doctrine shift is not into another single mode, but into one that requires continuous flexibility and adaptability.
